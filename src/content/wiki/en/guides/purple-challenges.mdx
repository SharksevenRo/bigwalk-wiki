## How to Unlock the Purple Door

The seven purple challenges live behind a purple door in the area behind the eastern cable car point. Finding that door is itself the first test of how well your group learned the island's [cable car network](/maps/getting-started/). Completing the challenges and forging the white key they lead to unlocks the [true ending](/guides/endings/). Come prepared: several challenges assume your group has mastered the [tools and toys](/items/getting-started/) — especially the whiteboard and flashlight.

![A mysterious purple-glowing puzzle chamber behind the secret door](/images/articles/bw-purple-challenges.jpg)

## The Seven Challenges

### 1. Wait 30 Minutes House

A house whose door simply requires the group to wait inside together for thirty real minutes. There is no trick, no timer skip — the challenge is whether your group can actually talk for half an hour. Veterans use it for a snack break. It's the game's quietest joke and a deliberate pacing valve.

### 2. Golf — But You Can't Throw

A golf-style objective where the ball may only be carried by hand and passed between players — throwing is disabled. Strategy: form a bucket brigade, call distances, and never let the ball carrier also be the navigator.

### 3. 36 Drawings

Thirty-six drawings must be placed on walls by teammates who cannot see the reference; one viewer calls them out. Brute-force descriptions fail — the intended solution is disciplined whiteboard work: viewer sketches, field players confirm, place, repeat. This is the longest single challenge.

### 4. Nine Speakers, But You Can't Talk

Nine speakers must be activated in the right order while the game mutes normal voice chat. Strategy: assign numbers via emotes or megaphone taps before entering, and use the radio sparingly — it still works, but treat it as a backup, not the plan.

### 5. Nine Symbols

A grid-matching challenge in the classic symbol family: one player sees the nine symbols, others place them. The [puzzle solutions guide](/guides/puzzle-solutions/) covers the general method — whiteboard plus radio confirmation.

### 6. Platforming, But Do It Fast

A timed platforming course. No communication trick here — pure execution. Let your group's most practiced platformer run it, with everyone else silent except hazard callouts. Checkpoint discipline beats speed.

### 7. Many Lights Everywhere

The finale: a field full of lights where normal speech is useless. The agreed protocol is flashlight morse — one flash means "yes," two flashes mean "no" — to answer a long sequence of binary questions while surrounded by noise. Slow down. A wrong answer costs more time than a careful double-check. This challenge is the reason the one-flash/two-flash convention exists, and it pays off directly at the [true ending](/guides/endings/).

## Rewards

The challenges feed the purple blorb chain that forges the white key — the key to the true ending. Collectively they also complete several of the game's 12 [achievements](/guides/achievements/).

## Group Tips

- Budget a full session for all seven; rushing the later ones is the top cause of failure.
- Designate one "caller" per challenge — democracy is slow when only one player can see the information.
- If voice gets heated, take the Wait 30 Minutes House first. It resets the mood, literally by design.
