## What Is Big Walk, Exactly?

Big Walk is a cooperative first-person adventure built around one idea: a big island, a group of friends, and no quest markers. There is no matchmaking — you play with people you invite via a join code — and only the host's world progresses. As of patch 1.4.8 (the October 2026 voice-chat fix), cross-play works across PC, Mac, PS5, and Switch 2. There is no Xbox version.

![A group of friends hikes across the open island toward a distant tower](/images/articles/bw-getting-started.jpg)

## How a Session Works

One player acts as the host. The host chooses how many towers the world spawns with (a 2-tower, 3-tower, or 4+ tower layout) and every save lives in the host's game. If you join a friend's island, your progress there only exists while the host keeps that world. Key things to know:

- **Join codes** are generated in the *Session Details* menu — the host shares it, friends enter it, done.
- **Group size** ranges from 2 up to 12 players, but puzzles are designed around splitting into smaller teams.
- **Progress belongs to the host.** If the host loses the save, the island is gone — treat the host machine carefully.

For what the island itself contains, see the [Big Walk island map overview](/maps/getting-started/).

## Your First Thirty Minutes

Expect to wander. That is the point. A typical opening session looks like this:

1. Spawn in, find your friends, and test voice chat immediately (proximity audio is the core mechanic — check [how voice chat works](/guides/voice-chat/) if anyone sounds too quiet).
2. Find the paper map. Someone should carry it and act as navigator.
3. Walk to the nearest tower. Towers are your progress checkpoints — everything in the main path revolves around them.
4. Pick up any [tools and toys](/items/getting-started/) you stumble on — the radio, flashlight, and whiteboard all solve real problems.

## Why There's No HUD

Big Walk deliberately hides everything a normal game would show you. No compass, no waypoint, no objective list. The designers want you to talk: "I see a bridge north of the cable car" is more valuable than any marker. If your group goes silent, the game gets hard fast — keep narrating what you see.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- **Wandering off alone without saying so.** Tell the group before you scout; the map is huge and people get lost.
- **Ignoring quiet teammates.** If someone is far away, their voice literally fades — that is proximity audio, not a bug.
- **Skipping the paper map.** Digital maps don't exist here; the hand-drawn one plus the in-game Map Room are all you get.
