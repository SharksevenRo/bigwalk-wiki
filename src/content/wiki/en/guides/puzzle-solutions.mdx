## The One Rule Behind Every Puzzle

Every puzzle in Big Walk separates *information* from *action*. One player can see the answer but can't operate the mechanism; another can operate it but can't see. The game is a communication test wearing an adventure game's clothes. Keep that in mind and no puzzle will ever feel unfair. Make sure your group has the [tools and toys](/items/getting-started/) covered before attempting the harder families.

## Remote Button Puzzles

The signature family. One player stands at a remote button; the other can reach the reward. The famous example: a puzzle numbered **4166/1899** — the solution is the island's coordinates, 41.66 by 18.99 (the island's proportions in kilometers). One player holds the button so the other can grab the red gourd behind the gate. Strategy: the button holder should describe their panel completely; the explorer reports what the gate is doing in real time.

## Symbol and Shape Puzzles

A nine-symbol family appears multiple times in the game: one player sees a grid of symbols, teammates see sockets that must be filled in matching order. The reliable method is the whiteboard: the observer sketches each symbol, confirms over the radio, and the field player places pieces. Shouting colors and shapes across a valley is where most groups fail — draw, don't describe.

## Light and Signal Puzzles

Several late-game challenges use the flashlight as a binary channel: one flash = yes, two flashes = no. These appear in the [purple post-game challenges](/guides/purple-challenges/), including one where a huge field of lights surrounds you and only disciplined flashing gets the message through. Agree on the protocol *before* you need it.

## Drawing Puzzles

One player views a reference image and directs others who place physical drawings on walls — the "36 Drawings" challenge is the purest form. The viewer must not touch the placement side; the placers can't see the reference. This one breaks groups that skip their whiteboard habits.

## Platforming and Speed Puzzles

A minority of Big Walk content is physical rather than communicative: timed platforming routes, including a post-game challenge that demands you complete a platforming course fast. These reward the one friend who plays platformers — let them run it while everyone else watches and calls out hazards.

## When Your Group Is Stuck

1. Re-check who can see what — someone is probably standing in the wrong place.
2. Swap roles; fresh eyes on the information side catch what tired ones miss.
3. Confirm your tools: puzzles often need the radio or whiteboard from the [items list](/items/getting-started/).
4. Still stuck? Cross-reference the [island map landmarks](/maps/getting-started/) — you may simply be at the wrong site.
