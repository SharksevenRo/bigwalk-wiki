## No Minimap, No Compass — What You Actually Get

Big Walk has no on-screen navigation UI at all. Instead, the game gives your group three layers of wayfinding: a physical paper map someone has to carry, a Map Room on the island with a large terrain model, and landmarks with distinct silhouettes you learn to name. Every successful navigation call in this game is a sentence like "meet at the east cable car" — which is exactly why voice chat matters (see [how voice chat works](/guides/voice-chat/)).

![An aerial view of the island: towers, cable car lines, and a hand-drawn map](/images/articles/bw-island-map.jpg)

## Towers: The Island's Spine

Towers are the most important structures on the map. The host chooses at world creation whether the island spawns with 2, 3, or 4+ towers, and that choice shapes the whole run:

- Each tower is a region's landmark and puzzle hub.
- The [main ending path](/guides/endings/) requires activating the towers, then traveling to the Black Obelisk.
- More towers = longer session, more puzzle variety.

## Cable Cars and Region Travel

Cable cars are the island's long-distance transit. Learning the cable car network is the single best navigation investment a group can make — shouting "take the east cable car" beats five minutes of "walk past the big rock, no the other rock." The true-ending [purple-door area](/guides/endings/) is even hidden behind the eastern cable car point, which rewards players who actually learn the network.

## The Map Room and Paper Map

The Map Room contains a large physical model of the island — your only "zoomed out" view of the world. The portable paper map is the field version: someone carries it, someone else calls out features, and together you triangulate. Groups that designate a navigator and a map-reader finish puzzles measurably faster; wandering pairs that both guess get lost.

## Landmarks and Giving Coordinates

Veteran groups quickly develop their own landmark names — "the antenna hill," "the twin pines." Combined with rough compass directions, this becomes the island's informal coordinate system. A few habits worth copying:

- Name every distinctive thing you find, out loud, once. Shared vocabulary saves hours.
- When separated, report a landmark plus a direction ("I'm at the bridge, south side").
- If truly lost, ask someone with the paper map to guide you in over the radio — that's what it's for, and the [tools list](/items/getting-started/) explains which items extend your sight range.

## Map FAQ

**Does the map change between sessions?**
The host's world layout (number of towers) persists in their save. Item and puzzle placement follows the chosen layout.

**Is there a fast travel system?**
Cable cars are the fast travel. Everything else is walking — deliberately.

**Where do I find the paper map?**
Early on, near spawn areas — but its exact spot is something we leave for your group to discover on the ground. This page intentionally stays spoiler-free on locations.
